About NASA

NASA (National Aeronautics and Space Administration) is the United States government agency responsible for space exploration, aeronautics research, and scientific discovery beyond Earth. It was established in 1958 and has since played a major role in advancing human understanding of space. NASA is known for launching satellites, sending robotic missions to other planets, and conducting research that helps improve technology on Earth as well. One of its biggest achievements was the Apollo program, which successfully landed humans on the Moon in 1969.

Today, NASA continues to lead major space missions such as the Mars rovers, the James Webb Space Telescope, and the Artemis program, which aims to return humans to the Moon and eventually explore Mars. The agency works with international partners and private companies like SpaceX to make space travel more efficient and advanced. NASA’s research also supports climate monitoring, Earth science, and aviation safety, making it important not only for space exploration but also for understanding and protecting our own planet.
